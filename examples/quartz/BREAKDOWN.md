## The hook (0–3 s)
A sky tilts down onto an Alpine village with a clock tower and cows (0.0–1.3 s). By 1.3 s, a huge white **1967** sits over
the village, and the caption types "In 1967, the most accurate wristwatch on Earth was Swiss." The picture is
a postcard of a winner. The open question is unspoken: *why tell me who was winning?*
At 6.3 s the answer starts with a hard cut to black. "Seventeen years later" fills a grid of watchmaker icons, and two
out of three go dark by 11 s. **The hook is a reversal: postcard → loss, 1967 → 1984, in 11 seconds.**

## What keeps people watching
- **A reversal at 6.3 s.** The opening sells a winner, and the hard cut to black says the winner lost. You stay to find out how.
- **A question said out loud at 45.0 s:** "So why didn't Switzerland win?" It sits halfway through and restarts attention.
- **Numbers you can see:** ×5 (24.7 s), ¥450,000 (37.7 s), 91 parts (49.3 s), ≈ CHF 50 (86.0 s), 100,000,000 (91.7 s).
  Every claim becomes an object or a counter, never just a word.
- **One idea mirrored:** 91 parts in hundreds of workshops (47–54 s) against 3 parts (54–60 s), and later 51 plastic parts (82.5 s).
  The cause of the loss is also the recipe for the comeback.
- **Two map worlds in two colours:** Switzerland red, Japan teal. You always know whose side a shot is on.
- **One still beat** at 69.3 s (the empty workshop, in black and white). Everything else moves, so the stillness lands as grief.

## The script
216 words in about 98 s of speech: 2.2 words/s, unhurried for short form, with gaps of 0.6–1.2 s between lines so each picture lands.
Structure: winner (0–6) → loss (6–12) → how they invented it (12–26) → how Japan sold it (26–45) → **why** (45–60) →
the collapse (60–75) → the comeback (75–94) → the line that closes the loop (94–100).
The line that does the most work: *"Every part it removed was somebody's living."* It turns an engineering fact into people.

## The ending
A callback: the last shot is the opening village. The last line reverses the first one ("most accurate wristwatch" →
"came back with a plastic one"). No call to action; the loop invites a rewatch.

## Steal this
1. **Open on the winner, cut to the loss.** Spend 5 s making the audience believe one thing, then flip it with a hard cut to black.
2. **Ask the viewer's question out loud at the halfway mark.** "So why didn't X win?" buys you the second half.
3. **Make every number an object.** A circled ×5, a price tag, a part counter, never a number on its own.
4. **Give each side a colour and keep it.** Here red is Switzerland and teal is Japan, on maps, charts and labels.
5. **End on the first image.** A callback with a reversed line closes the story and gets the rewatch.

## Don't copy
- The product photography (Astron, Swatch, movements) and the period maps took a production pipeline to make.
  Use the moves, not the assets.
- Brand names and real logos are fine in an editorial story; in an ad they can need permission.
